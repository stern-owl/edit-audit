## 0. Headline findings (abstract-level, with the numbers behind them)

1. **The draft reproduces exactly on different hardware.** A full re-run on A100 (draft: H100 MIG) gives identical
   Table 2, Table 3 and findings F1–F5; 2,646 per-path rows differ in no rank and no generated text (§1).
2. **The main finding generalises to 10× more facts, 7 relations, and a second model.** After a successful edit, the
   reverse read from the old object still returns the edited subject at rank 1 on **74–75% (MEMIT, AlphaEdit) of valid
   reverse paths on GPT-2 XL (78 facts) and 78% on GPT-J (128 facts)**, counting successful edits only. Of the reverse
   reads that returned the subject at rank 1 before the edit, **95–98% still do after it** under MEMIT and AlphaEdit on
   both models (§4.3, §5).
3. **The edit is not visible from the new object either.** On reverse-from-new paths the subject reaches rank 1 in
   **0 of 1,187** valid reads of successful edits (153 + 125 + 153 on GPT-2 XL, 252 × 3 on GPT-J); it becomes more likely
   in only 4–18%. Under the declared relation contract, **85–97%** of these reads are violations, **100%** for 1:1
   relations (capital, currency) under every method and model (§4.4).
4. **Standard specificity hides real drift.** Same-relation neighbours keep their correct answer on 94–98% of reads under
   MEMIT and AlphaEdit (both models) and 89% under ROME on GPT-2 XL, so a specificity check passes; yet among those passing
   reads the next-token distribution moves by more than 0.01 bit (Jensen–Shannon) on **20–33% (MEMIT, AlphaEdit) and 88–94%
   (ROME)**, and the new value enters a neighbour's top 5 in 4–10% (MEMIT, AlphaEdit) and 21% (ROME) on GPT-2 XL (§6).
5. **A single score can reward the worst configuration.** ROME in EasyEdit's default configuration (without covariance
   statistics) has the fewest stale reverse reads on GPT-J (30%), but only because it overwrites broadly: 48% of
   same-relation neighbour reads lose their correct answer and the new value becomes their top answer in 24% (50/207).
   With statistics (as in the original ROME), stale reads rise to 72% and the takeover falls to 3% (7/207): the same
   editor, configured to be local, leaves the reverse path stale like MEMIT and AlphaEdit (§3.1, §5.2). A stale-read rate
   alone would rank the damaging configuration best; the audit separates the two failures.
6. **ROME's difference from MEMIT/AlphaEdit in the draft is a statistics effect.** With covariance statistics ROME's
   forward profile becomes identical to AlphaEdit's, and its stale-read rate stays high (22/24) (§3).
7. **Sequential editing separates the methods sharply** (60 edits + 18 never-edited sentinels, GPT-2 XL, §7):
   - ROME collapses within 10–20 edits: after 20 edits only 50% of earlier edits hold, 7% of not-yet-edited facts keep
     their answer, untouched neighbour JS 0.77; after 60 edits 5/60 edits survive.
   - MEMIT degrades gradually: 38 of the 53 edits that took still hold after 60 edits; sentinel retention 72%.
   - AlphaEdit keeps **every** edit (60/60) but does **not** keep unrelated knowledge intact: sentinel retention falls
     to 83%, untouched same-relation neighbours keep their answer on 83%, their median JS rises from 0.0000 to 0.11.
   - Among edits that still hold, stale reverse reads stay at 62–77% throughout for MEMIT and AlphaEdit; ROME's 0% after
     collapse (0 of the 10 reverse paths of its 5 surviving edits) is not consistency.
8. **Standard evaluation passes edits the audit rejects, and the right questions are few** (phase 6 offline, entry 09,
   §7a; local configurations, 618 edits, both models): 441 edits pass CounterFact-style efficacy, paraphrase and
   neighbourhood scores; the audit flags **440** of them (380 as stale reads, which those scores cannot see). The
   six-question standard plan reveals 37% of inconsistent edits; **two reverse-path questions reveal 94%**, and an
   adaptive audit needs 1.26 questions per edit on average. For 1:1 relations every checked successful edit (66 / 66)
   leaves the model violating the inverse functional dependency, which an admission check on the request detects
   beforehand; stale reads are predicted by the pre-edit reverse state (recall 100%, precision 97%).
9. **Edits on related facts do not commute, and the failure is a lost update** (N1, entry 10, §7b; GPT-2 XL, 126
   pairs, both orders): edits on unrelated facts commute (0.1-0.2% of verdicts depend on the order), but when two edits
   share an entity the later one can silently revert the earlier one: **28% (ROME+stats) and 19% (MEMIT) of first edits
   in shared-object pairs are lost**, mostly because the second edit writes the first edit's old value. AlphaEdit loses
   none ("first writer wins"). Whether a pair conflicts is predictable before editing from the overlap of the two
   subjects' keys at the rewrite layers (Spearman ρ 0.87-0.94 with the order gap).
10. **Undo and repair are not what they seem** (N5, N2, entry 11, §7c; GPT-2 XL): a compensating edit (o* → o) brings the
    answer back in every case, but the old answer becomes more confident than before (95-99% of facts), the weights end
    further from the original than after the edit (1.08-1.63 ×), and neighbours stay disturbed: only a physical restore
    undoes an edit. A cascading repair of a one-to-one constraint (swap) restores it on the forward paths for AlphaEdit
    (9/9) but on the reverse paths for no method in no order (0/9); for ROME+stats the repair is itself a lost update.
11. **The audit found real bugs that standard pipelines did not:** a shipped analysis script that cannot run in its own
   documented environment, and an EasyEdit bug that makes AlphaEdit fail on GPT-J (§12).

## 8. Cross-cutting observations for the paper's argument
- **Forward success ⇏ reverse consistency** on two models, three methods, seven relations; strongest for the methods
  designed to be local (MEMIT, AlphaEdit): their pre-edit rank-1 reverse reads survive the edit 95–98% of the time.
- **Locality and consistency trade off** across configurations: ROME without covariance statistics breaks more reverse
  reads (survival 37% on GPT-J) only by corrupting neighbours (48% lose their correct answer; 24% now answer with the new
  value); the same editor with statistics is local and leaves 88% of the reverse reads stale. Every *local* configuration
  we ran (ROME+stats, MEMIT, AlphaEdit, both models) leaves 88–98% of pre-edit rank-1 reverse reads untouched.
- **1:1 relations make the problem crisp:** the edit request already violates the declared constraint, and the audit shows
  the reverse index is never updated (0% rank 1, 100% violations in every run).
- **Specificity at rank 1 is a weak test:** a quarter to a third of "passing" neighbours shift measurably under MEMIT and
  AlphaEdit, almost all under ROME.
- **Over edit sequences**, the three methods differ in kind: collapse (ROME), gradual decay (MEMIT), edits preserved while
  unrelated knowledge erodes (AlphaEdit). A transaction-style audit after every write (or at checkpoints) makes this visible;
  a one-time evaluation at the end would not show when and how it happened.

## 13. Limitations and threats to validity (current)
- **Seeds:** E4/E5/E7 seed 0 only. In E0–E2 (3 seeds) rank-1 verdicts agreed across seeds for almost all paths.
- **Facts:** 187 hand-written facts unverified by a person; CounterFact P36 subjects include historical states.
- **Screening bias:** first-token-rank screening excludes answers after articles (paintings on GPT-2 XL, currencies on
